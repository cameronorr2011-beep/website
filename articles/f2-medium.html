Two millilitres per litre. That is the whole method.

f/2 is the enrichment medium behind almost every marine microalgae culture, and it is simple enough to be dosed with a syringe. Most failures blamed on the medium are actually failures of the water or the dose arithmetic.

18 November 2026Cameron Orr7 min read

f/2 is the medium published by Guillard and Ryther in 1962, and it is still the standard because it is cheap, stable and forgiving. Nitrate for nitrogen, phosphate for phosphorus, a chelated iron source, a handful of trace metals, and three vitamins. The "f" is for the fraction — half-strength of the older f medium — and the recipe has not changed in any way that matters to a hatchery.

We make it as a single acidic liquid concentrate at 500×. One millilitre treats 500 mL of culture, two treat a litre, and there is no weighing, no pH adjustment and no bottle of precipitate on the shelf.

How to use it

  1. Make the seawater. Marine salt at 30 parts per thousand in distilled or RO water. Never tap water — chlorination is designed to kill exactly what you are trying to grow, and it will.
  2. Dose the medium into the water, then inoculate. Two millilitres per litre, swirled in before the culture goes in. Adding concentrate directly against a dense inoculum gives the cells a local burn.
  3. Dose again at every split. The medium is consumed as the culture grows. A split into fresh seawater without fresh f/2 is a dilution, not a feeding, and the culture will yellow within days.
  4. Halve the dose for a maintenance culture. If you are holding a strain rather than growing volume — a backup on the shelf that you split monthly — half-strength keeps it alive at a slower division rate without over-shooting the nutrient.

Why liquid concentrate, and not dry salts

f/2 works because EDTA keeps iron in solution where a cell can take it up. Mix the dry salts straight into seawater and the iron precipitates as an insoluble phosphate before anything can use it: the water goes cloudy, the culture stalls, and the iron is sitting on the bottom of the vessel. A single-phase acidic concentrate holds the chelate intact, so a bottle opened in March still works in November.

There is a second, less scientific reason. Sodium nitrate is an oxidiser and a listed explosives precursor, and shipping it as dry powder in consumer mail is a conversation with a carrier that nobody wants to have. Dilute liquid is not a problem.

Silicate: when to ask for it

Standard f/2 omits silicate on purpose. Diatoms need it to build their walls, so silicate in a flagellate culture is an invitation to exactly the contamination you are trying to avoid — a Nannochloropsis batch with silicate in it will eventually grow something with a shell.

For Chaetoceros muelleri and Thalassiosira pseudonana it is mandatory. Order the f/2 plus silicate variant with the strain and dose it identically; without it the culture divides twice and stops, and no amount of extra nitrate fixes a silica limitation.

Reading a dose problem

  • Pale yellow-green at the meniscus by day four — under-dosed or overdue for a split. Dilute 1:1 into fresh seawater with a full dose and it usually recovers.
  • Deep, dark green that will not clear — the culture has out-grown its light, not its nutrient. Thin it rather than feeding it.
  • Cloudy water with a film — not a medium problem. That is bacterial or algal contamination and the culture should be discarded.
  • Slow growth from day one — check salinity and temperature before blaming the bottle. A culture at 15 °C grows at half the speed of one at 22 °C, and a culture made with tap water may not grow at all.

Slight over-dosing is harmless to the algae, within reason; the cost is that unused nutrient feeds whatever else is in the vessel. Under-dosing is the more common error, because people dose once and then split three times without adding more.

Storage and handling

Store dark at room temperature. Shelf life is twelve months from mixing, and a bottle that has been frozen or left in a hot car should be replaced rather than risked on a spawn. The concentrate is acidic: rinse the syringe or dropper after use, keep it out of reach of children, and do not drink any of it. It is a plant nutrient, not a food, and nothing in it is for human consumption.
