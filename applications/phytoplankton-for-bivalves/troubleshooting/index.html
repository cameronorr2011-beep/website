Applications · Bivalves / Failure modes

Troubleshooting.

Most failures announce themselves early. The bench skill is reading which failure you have before the tank decides for you.

Common failure modes

SymptomLikely causeBench response
Algal culture turns brown/crashingContamination, light or nutrient exhaustionRestart from reserve stock; never “rescue” into larvae
Larvae clear none of the rationCiliates/rotifers grazing the dose, or larvae stressedSiphon immediately; check counts; consider a water change
Sudden overnight mortalityTemperature spike, oxygen crash, ammonia spikeAudit heater, aeration and last water change — usually environmental, not disease
Shells malform or stay thinpH/alkalinity off, or trace metal issueTest pH and salinity; remove any brass/copper; RO water + proper salt mix
Larvae never settle onto spat cultchWrong temperature/salinity window or unconditioned cultchMatch species’ spawning window; use conditioned shell or spat catchers
Progressive mortality over daysBacterial vibrio loadStop feeding, exchange 50%+ water, consult local hatchery/Extension — home fixes are limited

The honest limit

Beyond basic husbandry, larval culture involves bacterial disease, genetic broodstock quality and regulatory disease controls that no home bench can properly resolve. Use your national fisheries institute or local extension service for persistent failure; ask for pathogen screening, not folklore.

Biosecurity

Never move larvae, cultch or broodstock between estuaries or states without approval; pathogens such as Perkinsus and Bonamia have devastated fisheries across borders. Hobby practice belongs in contained systems with purchased seed of known provenance.

Sources

  1. Helm MM, Bourne N, Lovatelli A. Hatchery culture of bivalves — A practical manual. FAO Fisheries Technical Paper No. 471, Rome, 2004. FAO — disease, mortality and husbandry baselines.
  2. Your national fisheries / aquaculture extension service — binding rules and local pathogen advice.
