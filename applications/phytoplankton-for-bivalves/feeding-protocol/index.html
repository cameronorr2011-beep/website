The feeding protocol.

Feeding is a closed loop: measure the larvae, feed what they clear, keep the tank clean. Guessing cell counts is where most hobby failures begin.

The loop

  1. Count larvae. Sample a measured volume daily; estimate larvae per mL. Sudden drops signal water problems, not hunger.
  2. Feed to clearance. A common practice is dosing algae so most of it is cleared within a few hours of feeding; if the tank is still brightly green at the next feed, halve the dose. Target algal densities are strain- and stage-dependent — suppliers publish cell/mL; working range for practical purposes is on the order of 106–107 cells/mL at feeding.
  3. Feed twice daily for fast-growing warmwater larvae; once daily can suffice for coldwater species.
  4. Siphon daily. Exchange 30–50% of tank volume every 1–2 days, siphoning larval settlement debris — bacterial load, not food, is what usually crashes young larvae.
  5. Log everything. Date, ration, water temperature, counts, mortality. The notebook is the instrument.

Culture management

Maintain at least two algal stock cultures in continuous or semi-continuous culture; a third on standby covers crashes. T-ISO in 1–2 L flasks with gentle aeration and cool-white fluorescent light is the classic hobby setup; scale up by weekly doubling, never by sudden 5× jumps.

What not to do

Do not dose “clear tank” doses of algae continuously; do not feed rotten or stagnant algal culture; do not skip water exchange because the tank “looks clean”; do not raise larval densities beyond what your algal capacity feeds comfortably.

Scope. This is an educational routine adapted from published hatchery practice, not veterinary or production certification. Follow local regulations on culturing and releasing bivalves.

Sources

  1. Helm MM, Bourne N, Lovatelli A. Hatchery culture of bivalves — A practical manual. FAO Fisheries Technical Paper No. 471, Rome, 2004. FAO — rationing, siphoning and larval husbandry.
