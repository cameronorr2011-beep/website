Inventions · The workshop

The workshop.

Orr Biologicals’ own engineering — instruments and methods aimed at biology’s hardest measurement problems. Two projects. Both open. Both in development. Nothing on this page is a product, and nothing here implies one.

I—01 · Algaephyte

Open-source systems for understanding and monitoring microalgal cultures.

The problem. Cultures fail quietly. pH, light, temperature and nutrients drift together, and by the time the jar looks wrong the experiment is already lost. Algaephyte asks whether affordable sensing, a camera and an honest growth model — with human-enforced limits — can turn silent drift into an observable, reproducible signal.

Proposed architecture — not implemented hardwareDiagram: original, MIT-licensed

Exists today

  • The design rationale and software-model work, published in the open.
  • The Algal Digital Lab — a working, browser-based cultivation simulator.
  • BioRealm — the field-study game that trains cultivation intuition (noindex experiment).

Proposed, not built

  • Off-the-shelf sensor integration (pH, temperature, optical density, light) on a logging backbone.
  • A physics-informed growth model (Droop–Steele family) fitted to honest culture data.
  • Camera-assisted vitality checks with a human sign-off gate for any actuation.

Planned before claims

  • A documented bench build with photographs, a bill of materials and known failures.
  • A validation protocol against blinded, separate observations.
  • MIT-licensed build sheets — only after the above, never before.

Development status, plainly. No bench prototype exists. No measurement has been validated. No performance number belongs in this document. The Algal Digital Lab is a simulation; the game is a simulation. Both are honest about it.

Known limitations. Growth models fit populations, not your particular culture; sensors drift; the most valuable output may simply be a better logbook. None of these are fatal — all are stated, because silence is not a feature.

Where this goes. 1 — simulation fidelity against published reactor literature; 2 — one boring, documented bench build; 3 — open validation data; 4 — build sheets, if the build survives its own notes.

Reference points. Schagerl et al. (2022), biomass and vitality estimation (PMC9368473); Benner et al. (2022), lab-scale photobioreactors (PMC9033726); Chanquia et al., single-cell methods (elsc.202100070) — literature, not our results. Full project page: Algaephyte.

I—02 · Cyanoflow

Open-source tools for studying biological variation at the single-cell scale.

The problem. Bulk assays average. In a culture crash or a selection experiment, the interesting biology often lives in the minority — the stressed cell, the survivor, the outlier. Cyanoflow asks whether affordable microfluidics and accessible imaging can make single-cell observation legible to working scientists outside core facilities.

Proposed pipeline — not fabricated hardwareDiagram: original, MIT-licensed

Exists today

  • The interactive single-cell concept page — the working demonstration of the approach.
  • Single-cell imaging brief: droplets vs microwells for microalgae.
  • The Algal Digital Lab for culture-scale context and teaching.

Proposed, not built

  • Microfluidic chips for cell isolation and ordered imaging (design goals, not layouts under NDA).
  • A tracking-and-phenotyping pipeline with explicit uncertainty, not vibes.
  • Blinded validation against known strains before any claim of detection.

Planned before claims

  • Fabrication-first reality check: channel geometry, bonding, fouling, cost per chip.
  • A blind two-strain test, reported with all failures included.
  • Open CAD/layout release — only when it survives the above.

Development status, plainly. We have not fabricated a device. We have not validated detection. The single-cell page is a demonstration of intent and interface, honest about its own limits.

Known limitations. Fouling, clogging and drift defeat most first chips; imaging quantifies variation but does not identify species; the field’s best results come from patient, boring replication.

Where this goes. 1 — chip design notes and a fabrication attempt, photographed; 2 — blind validation, reported fully; 3 — open toolchain, documented honestly; 4 — build sheets, if the chips deserve them.

Reference points. Chanquia et al., single-cell methods (elsc.202100070); Schagerl et al. (2022), vitality proxies (PMC9368473) — literature, not our results. Project page: Cyanoflow.

Why these two

Algaephyte watches the culture; Cyanoflow watches the cell. Both ask the same question — can open engineering make biology legible? Neither asks you to trust us; both ask you to read the logbook. Future inventions will appear here only when they meet the same bar: documented, open, honest, or not published.

Licensing

Site text, diagrams and code are MIT-licensed. When hardware designs exist, they will be released under a permissive hardware licence with attribution. No patent fencing, no closed forks of public work. See the license.

A note on honesty

Neither invention has been built as a validated instrument. Illustrative renderings are labelled as such everywhere they appear. If you need a validated system today, do not wait for us — buy from whoever already ships one, and ask them the hard question about validation instead.
