First week

What you actually do

Five steps, about twenty minutes on day one and five minutes a day after that.

Mix the water

Fill the carboy with tap water and stir in the salt. Check it reads 30 ppt with a refractometer or hydrometer, then let it stand until it's clear. If you don't have a meter, 1000 g into three litres of water is close enough to start.

Add f/2 and the culture

Dose 2 mL of f/2 per litre, swirl, then pour in the 16 oz bottle. Put the cap on with the airline through it and switch the pump on. It should bubble gently, not foam.

Give it light

Put the LED a hand's width from the carboy and run it 12 to 16 hours a day. A timer helps. Keep it out of direct sun, which cooks a gallon quickly.

Check the colour

By day three it should be greener than when you started. Lower the Secchi stick until you can't see the disc; the depth is your density reading. If nothing changes by day five, something is wrong.

Split it

When the Secchi reading gets shallow and the culture is dark, pour half into a clean bottle with fresh saltwater and f/2. That's the whole method — keep splitting and you keep feeding.

What isn't in the box

A refractometer, a heater and a spare bottle. You don't need the first two to start, and the third you can improvise with a clean drinks bottle. I'd rather leave them out than raise the price by $30 for things you may already own.

Questions

Before you buy

The five things people email about.

How long until it looks green?

Three to five days at room temperature with light and air. If it's still pale after a week, check the salinity, the light and whether the pump is actually moving water.

Do I need the air pump?

Yes, for a gallon. Without movement the cells settle into a dead layer at the bottom within a day or two. The included pump is sized for exactly this volume.

How much water does the salt make?

1000 g makes 28–30 litres at 30 ppt. The carboy is a gallon, so you'll mix a gallon and keep the rest sealed for the first split.

What if the culture crashes?

Send me a photo and the Secchi reading. Usually it's too much light, no air, or a contaminated split. If the bottle arrived dead, it's replaced.
