What it looks like set up

A gallon carboy, a small pump, a light bar and a stick with a disc on the end. That's the whole apparatus.

The first week

Set-up takes about ten minutes. After that it's a glance and a note until day seven, when the culture is thick enough to split.

  1. Mix the salt water

    Dissolve the 1000 g of salt in roughly 28–30 liters of distilled or RO water, not tap. That's 30 ppt, the strength these organisms grow at. You only need a gallon today; keep the rest covered.

  2. Fill the carboy and dose

    Fill the carboy with the salt water. Add f/2 at 2 mL per liter, about 7.5 mL for a gallon. The 50 mL bottle treats roughly 25 liters.

  3. Add the culture

    Pour in the whole 16 oz bottle. Swirl the carboy gently. It will look pale for a few days; that's normal.

  4. Set the air and the light

    Put the pump and airline in, turned low enough to move the water without splashing. Set the LED beside it. Twelve hours on, twelve off is the schedule.

  5. Read the Secchi stick

    After a week, lower the stick in and note where the disc disappears. Write the number down. Next week's number is the whole point.

What's not in the box

Three things, deliberately, because they either add weight or add a hazard without adding a cell of algae.

Water

Three liters of distilled water weighs three kilograms. Buy it locally for about a dollar. Tap water is the one thing that will kill this culture quickly, so don't use it.

A timer

A plug-in timer costs about $8 and is the best accessory you can add. Twelve hours of light is not something people remember to do by hand for a month.

A mains-powered pump or light

Everything runs from USB. Water and wall voltage in the same experiment is a risk this kit doesn't need to take.

Handling

The f/2 concentrate is acidic. Rinse the dropper after use, keep it away from small children, and don't drink it. Nothing in the kit is food, and none of it is for human consumption.
