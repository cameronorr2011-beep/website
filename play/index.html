Home cultures · Play

Three small games
for a quiet bench.

No accounts, no leaderboard, nothing to install. Three short puzzles about the words, the order of work and the judgement calls that come with keeping a culture alive. Everything runs in this page; nothing is sent anywhere.

How it works

No timer, no account, no tracking. Every control is a native button, link or radio button, so the page works with a keyboard and a screen reader.

About five minutes for all three. A wrong answer explains itself rather than just marking you down.

Game 01 / memory

Six pairs. Turn two cards at a time.

Bench match.

Six terms, six explanations. Turn two cards; if they belong together, they stay face up. The cards are buttons, so this works with a keyboard: Tab to a card, then Enter or Space to turn it.

    Game 02 / sequence

    Place all five steps.

    A week in order.

    Five steps for starting a sourdough starter, shuffled. Choose them in the order you would actually do them, then check. Choosing is not final: use Remove to take a step back.

        Game 03 / judgement

        Five questions

        Read the jar.

        A short quiz on the signals a home culture gives you. Each answer comes with the reasoning, including where appearance stops being enough. This is practice for reading a guide, not a diagnostic tool.

        01A starter has a domed top, even bubbles and a sour-milk smell, six hours after feeding.

        Ready. A predictable rise, a domed top and a fresh sour smell are the signals a starter is active. Keep feeding the same rhythm and it should keep repeating.

        02There is a layer of dark liquid on top and the starter has not risen.

        Hungry. That liquid is often called hooch. It means the culture has run out of food, not that it has died. Discard most of it, feed, and give it a few days to recover strength.

        03You see pink-orange streaks and a fuzzy patch on the surface.

        Discard. Fuzzy growth and pink or orange discolouration are not flavour. Do not try to rescue the culture by removing the visible part; throw it away and clean the jar.

        04A starter has been in the fridge for a month. It is thin, watery and slow to bubble after one feed.

        Overdue. Cold and neglect slow a starter down. Pour off any liquid, discard most of the culture and feed twice a day for several days before trusting it to raise bread.

        05A culture smells sharply of acetone, with no discolouration and no fuzzy growth.

        Neither proof. A sharp smell often follows a stall or over-fermentation. Feed consistently and keep observing; if it persists alongside discolouration, discard. Smell alone is not a diagnosis.

        These puzzles are teaching aids built from the site's guides. They are not a food-safety test, and a high score does not make a culture safe to eat. When in doubt, discard it.
