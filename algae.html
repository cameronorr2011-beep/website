The strains

Pick the cell that fits the stage

Every bottle holds 16 oz of actively dividing culture, counted the day before it ships. The micrograph on each card is the cell shape you'll see under a scope, not a stock photo. Each strain is $15 with its f/2.

Nannochloropsis oculata

2–3 µm · green · the beginner's strain

The one I hand someone who has never grown algae. Small round cells, dense green, and happy in a gallon with f/2. Feeds rotifers and brine shrimp.

Best forRotifer and brine shrimp cultures, green water, a first culture. Not a larval feed on its own — no DHA was detected in it, and bivalve larvae reject it when they can.

Micrograph (cropped): H. Kargin / Israeli J. Aquaculture, CC BY 4.0.

Isochrysis aff. galbana (T-Iso)

4–6 µm · golden-brown · DHA-rich

The base feed in most oyster hatcheries. Rich in DHA, fast to divide, and flagellated, so it stays in the water column with gentle air instead of settling out.

Best forOyster, clam, mussel and scallop larvae and spat, DHA enrichment for rotifers, and pairing with a small diatom — mixed diets grow larvae faster than any single species.

Nutrient media

50 mL of f/2, in every box

f/2 is the food: nitrate, phosphate, trace metals and vitamins, concentrated so you dose 2 mL per litre. The 50 mL bottle treats about 25 litres — a gallon batch plus a couple of splits. It comes with every culture, and a spare 50 mL bottle is $10 — a bottle of algae without food is dead in a week.

Marine salt

1000 g of salt, $5 more

Enough to mix about 33 litres at 30 g per litre. A gallon takes 114 g, so the bag covers the first batch and several splits. Fill a bucket with tap water, stir the salt in, check it with a refractometer, let it clear, then add the f/2. It's the same salt the cultures are grown in. $5 on its own, or $20 with a culture and its f/2. If you need both, the $13 salt + f/2 bundle is the cheaper way.

  • MakesAbout 33 litres at 30 g per litre.
  • Weight1000 g, sealed bag.
