The strains

Pick the cell that fits the stage

Every bottle holds 16 oz of actively dividing culture, counted the day before it ships. The micrograph on each card is the cell shape you'll see under a scope, not a stock photo. Each strain is $15 with its f/2.

Nannochloropsis oculata

2–3 µm · green · the beginner's strain

The one I hand someone who has never grown algae. Small round cells, dense green, and happy in a gallon with f/2. Feeds rotifers, brine shrimp and early larvae.

Micrograph (cropped): H. Kargin / Israeli J. Aquaculture, CC BY 4.0.

Isochrysis aff. galbana (T-Iso)

4–6 µm · golden-brown · DHA-rich

The base feed in most oyster hatcheries. Rich in DHA, fast to divide, and flagellated, so it stays in the water column with gentle air instead of settling out.

Micrograph (cropped): Olivier Dugornay / Ifremer / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0.

$15 with 50 mL f/2

Pavlova lutheri

4–6 µm · DHA + EPA · scallop and mussel work

Carries DHA and EPA in one cell, which is why scallop and mussel programmes use it. Slower and less forgiving than T-Iso, so it's usually part of the ration, not all of it.

Epifluorescence micrograph (cropped): Lim et al. 2012 / PLOS ONE, CC BY 4.0.

$15 with 50 mL f/2

Chaetoceros muelleri

4–6 µm · chain diatom · needs silicate

A silica-walled chain diatom for late larvae and post-set. It needs silicate in the medium, so order the f/2 with Si or the culture stalls after a couple of divisions.

Epifluorescence micrograph (cropped): Lim et al. 2012 / PLOS ONE, CC BY 4.0.

Thalassiosira pseudonana (3H)

3–5 µm · centric diatom · early larvae

A small centric diatom that fits early larvae where a chain would be too big. Also silicate-dependent, and quick to be outcompeted if you don't clean between batches.

SEM (cropped): P. Gröger et al. / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

Tetraselmis suecica

7–10 µm · green · late larvae and broodstock

Large, green and robust. A late-larval and broodstock feed, and a standard rotifer enrichment. Too big for a first-feeding veliger, which is the mistake it gets bought for.

Micrograph (cropped): Nobody16w / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

Nutrient media

50 mL of f/2, in every box

f/2 is the food: nitrate, phosphate, trace metals and vitamins, concentrated so you dose 2 mL per litre. The 50 mL bottle treats about 25 litres — a gallon batch plus a couple of splits. It comes with every culture and isn't sold on its own, because a bottle of algae without food is dead in a week.

  • Dose2 mL per litre of culture.
  • 50 mLTreats roughly 25 litres.
  • IncludedOne bottle with every 16 oz culture.
  • Not sold aloneIt ships with the culture, not as a separate product.

Marine salt

1000 g of salt, $5 more

Enough to mix 28–30 litres at 30 ppt. Fill a bucket with tap water, stir the salt in, check it with a refractometer, let it clear, then add the f/2. It's the same salt the cultures are grown in, and it only comes inside the $20 box — the $15 bottle is for people who already keep saltwater.

  • Makes28–30 litres at 30 ppt.
  • Weight1000 g, sealed bag.
  • Price$20 with the culture and f/2, never on its own.
  • MixingDissolves clear in a few minutes of stirring.

Why 16 oz and not a litre

A 16 oz bottle is enough to seed a gallon and split it a week later.
