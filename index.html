What bivalve larvae actually eat

Cell size is the constraint. A newly set veliger can only capture particles a few micrometres across, so the first feed is always the small flagellates — the diatoms come later, when the larvae can handle them.

Live culture, or paste?

Paste and concentrates earn their place in a hatchery: they keep for months, they need no light, and a busy week does not depend on a culture not crashing. Live earns its place differently.

A live culture is a seed, not a ration

Paste is feed. You buy it, you use it, you buy more. A live culture is feed plus the ability to make more feed — one 16 oz bottle and four litres of seawater becomes a working culture in a week, and doubles the week after, without another purchase. For a hatchery that loses a batch at 2 a.m. before a spawn, that is the difference between a delay and a lost set.

Live also lets you verify what you are feeding. Motility, colour and a cell count under a haemocytometer tell you the culture is alive and unialgal. A paste tells you what the label says.

Where paste wins, honestly

If you feed 500 litres a day, buy concentrate by the drum; nobody grows that in bottles, and this bench cannot compete with a commercial algal paste plant on price per cell. We are a bench-scale producer and we say so.

What we are good for is the other end: a research spawn, a restoration project with forty broodstock, a classroom, a quarantine tank, or a hatchery that wants a backup strain in the fridge before the busy season rather than during it.

Every batch is counted before it leaves. If a culture arrives dead, photograph it within 24 hours and it is replaced — that is the whole warranty, and it is the only one that matters with live material.

How an order runs

There is no shopping cart for hatchery cultures, because volume, strain and timing change the price. The whole process is four steps and one email.

  1. Tell us what you are feeding

    Species, stage, how many litres you feed per day, and the date you need it. If you are mid-spawn, say so — timing beats paperwork.

  2. We confirm strain and ship date

    You get a written quote with cell count, volume, price, and the exact Monday-to-Wednesday window the box will leave in. Nothing is charged until you approve it.

  3. We grow the batch

    Seven days at 20–24 °C, 12:12 light, gentle air. The day before shipping the culture is counted and checked for contamination, then split into the shipping vessel at 80% fill.

  4. It ships insulated, and it arrives alive

    Priority Mail, gel pack, double bag, absorbent pad, red live-culture label. If the destination forecast is above 32 °C we hold the order and tell you rather than ship into a heatwave.

Every batch is counted before it ships.

A haemocytometer count, a colour check and a contamination screen on the day of packing. The number goes on the label, because a cell count you cannot see is a claim, not a specification.

Do you ship live microalgae to hatcheries outside California?

Yes, within the United States. Live cultures ship Monday to Wednesday by USPS Priority Mail in an insulated box with a gel pack, so nothing sits in a depot over a weekend. We do not ship live cultures internationally, because customs will destroy them.

What strains can I order?

Nannochloropsis oculata is in weekly production. Isochrysis aff. galbana (T-Iso), Pavlova lutheri, Chaetoceros muelleri, Thalassiosira pseudonana and Tetraselmis suecica are also sold as 16 oz live cultures.

How much does hatchery algae cost?

Every culture is the same price: $15 for a 16 oz bottle with 50 mL of f/2, or $20 with 1,000 g of marine salt. There is no per-litre price because there are no litre batches. Order as many bottles as you need and the price per bottle stays the same.

What is f/2 medium and how much do I dose?

f/2 is the Guillard and Ryther (1962) enrichment medium used for nearly all marine microalgae culture. We ship it as a 500× liquid concentrate dosed at 2 mL per litre of culture, so one litre of concentrate treats 500 litres of seawater.

Can a beginner keep a culture alive?

Yes, if you start with one bottle and split it weekly. The $25 culture kit exists for exactly that: a one gallon carboy, a 16 oz live culture, 50 mL of f/2, 1,000 g of marine salt, a submersible air pump, airline, an LED light and a Secchi stick so you can measure growth instead of guessing.

Hobbyists, reef keepers, teachers and students buy the same bottles, and some add the $25 kit for the carboy, pump and light.
