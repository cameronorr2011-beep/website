Frequently asked questions

Short answers about both platforms. If yours isn't here, the troubleshooting guide covers failures, and the docs cover builds.

01What exactly are AlgaePhyte and Mycophyte?

AlgaePhyte (formerly MAIN) is an open-source, AI-assisted photobioreactor for growing Spirulina â€” it senses pH, temperature, light, optical density, dissolved oxygen and conductivity, models the culture with a digital twin, and proposes safe control actions. Mycophyte is the newer sibling: an open-source AI-assisted mushroom grow chamber with a target bill of materials under $300. Both are educational, open-source projects â€” they are not consumer appliances or medical devices.

02Is the AI really in control?

The AI proposes; rules and a simulated twin verify; you decide what to allow. Every control action must clear a deterministic rule gate and a forward simulation of the chamber or reactor before it can be applied. If anything is uncertain, the system fails closed â€” it does nothing.

03Do the systems work offline?

Yes. Sensing, modeling, prediction and actuation all run on the device itself. The network is used only for telemetry, federated model updates and (optionally) remote monitoring. If the network drops, the chamber or reactor simply keeps running its local loops.

04What data leaves the device?

By default, nothing. Sensor logs, camera frames and history stay on-device. If you opt into the federated network, only signed, bounds-checked model parameter updates are published â€” never raw readings or images. See Privacy for details.

05How is humidity controlled in Mycophyte?

The chamber closes the loop on relative humidity using a fogger (up), a fan (down) and a heater (which also dries). The controller watches dew point to avoid condensation on the walls and fruit, and pulses rather than runs actuators continuously.

The core set: temperature + humidity, COâ‚‚, and airflow. Optional: a camera for the experimental vision module, a load cell for substrate weight, and per-shelf mini-climate sensors.

Yes. COâ‚‚ is modeled from airflow and a default respiration estimate, and the control loop runs on fans alone. Adding the sensor turns COâ‚‚ control on; everything degrades gracefully.

08What sensors does AlgaePhyte need?

pH, temperature, light, optical density (OD), dissolved oxygen and conductivity (TDS). All are standard analog or IÂ²C probes; the docs list tested part numbers and wiring.

09What camera does the vision module use?

A standard USB or ESP32-CAM module. Inference runs on-device (optionally on a small USB accelerator). The detector flags anomalies for human review â€” it does not diagnose mold or contamination. Every flag includes the stored image and surrounding telemetry.

10What does 'federated learning' mean here?

Devices share only model parameter updates â€” not data. The aggregator averages updates into a shared twin model and distributes it back. Updates are signed and bounds-checked. It is not magic: privacy improves but is not absolute, and the aggregator is a trust point. Details in this article.

11How accurate are the simulations on this site?

The browser simulations run the same physics models as the firmware (Steele light curve, Beer-Lambert attenuation, bell-shaped temperature/pH responses, hygrometric chamber dynamics). They are illustrative â€” they show how the control logic behaves, not validated experimental data.

12Do you claim specific yields or harvest weights?

No. We deliberately do not publish yield or growth-rate numbers that we cannot back with rigorous, controlled data. The goal is control repeatability: the conditions you set are the conditions that hold. Any claims we add later will cite real experiments.

13What does it cost to build?

Mycophyte's target bill of materials is under $300 (not counting the chamber itself). AlgaePhyte is a Raspberry Pi-class build with hobby-grade sensors; the docs include full BOM tables with tested part numbers.

14What microcontrollers are supported?

AlgaePhyte runs on a Raspberry Pi (the twin model is a few hundred kilobytes). Mycophyte's core loop runs on RP2040-class boards; the camera path uses a small USB accelerator. Both use MQTT for telemetry.

15Can I grow other organisms with these systems?

The hardware is organism-agnostic; the models are not. The digital twin parameters are tuned per organism and per strain, and must be re-calibrated for anything else. The safety gates will reject actions outside the calibrated envelope.

16Is this food-safe? Is it a medical device?

Neither system is certified food-grade or a medical device. Growing food requires you to follow local food-safety rules, and we publish explicit food-safety guidance. The vision module is an experimental flagging aid, not a contamination clearance system.

17What happens if a sensor fails?

The controller detects implausible readings and enters a degraded mode: it stops closed-loop control on that channel and alerts you. Failsafe rules keep actuators in safe positions. See Troubleshooting.

18How do I reset the system?

Physical power-cycle resets the device; a long-press on the onboard button clears the paired network state. Firmware updates are applied over USB or OTA with a signed image; the previous version is always kept for rollback.

19Can I monitor remotely?

Yes, if you choose: the device publishes MQTT telemetry to a broker you configure (or our reference broker). Monitoring is read-only by default; control commands require your authentication.

20Where do I start?

Read the docs, try the simulations on the Mycophyte and AlgaePhyte pages, then pick a starter BOM. The community answers build questions via contact.
