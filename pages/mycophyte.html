Edge AI Â· on-device

The intelligence lives in the chamber, not the cloud

A small quantized model â€” hundreds of kilobytes, not gigabytes â€” runs on the microcontroller or a pocket-sized accelerator. It infers the chamber state from the sensor stream and proposes control actions locally.

The experimental vision module uses a lightweight object detector from the YOLO family to flag potential contamination â€” surface discoloration, unexpected growth patterns, liquid pooling â€” as prompts for human review. It does not diagnose mold; it tells you where to look. Detection is experimental and its reliability depends on your lighting, camera angle, and strain.

Offline is not a failure mode: if the network drops, a deterministic rule-based controller keeps the climate in range until the twin reconnects. The AI proposes; the rules enforce; the hardware fails closed.

Inference stack (per node)
  • Quantized twin: ~300â€“800 KB on-device
  • Camera module: YOLO-class detector, frames processed locally
  • Optional TPU accelerator for higher camera throughput
  • MQTT publish: model updates + telemetry, TLS where supported
  • Failsafe: rule-based controller, independent of the model
Why edge, not cloud

Latency matters for condensation control; privacy matters for your growing experiments; cost matters at lab scale. On-device inference keeps all three local.

Federated learning Â· MQTT

Every chamber teaches the fleet

Chambers connect over MQTT to a local broker or the open network. What travels between nodes is twin parameter updates â€” not your sensor logs, not your camera frames. The network learns strain-specific strategies from many growers without anyone's private data leaving their chamber.

What's shared
  • Model weight/parameter updates, signed and versioned
  • Anonymized outcome summaries (harvest timing, yield bands)
  • Firmware hashes for reproducible builds
What stays local
  • Raw sensor logs, camera frames, and local history
  • Network topology and device identities
  • Anything you haven't explicitly opted to publish

Honest limits: federated learning reduces data exposure; it is not a privacy guarantee. Standard cryptanalysis assumptions and your own threat model apply. See How it works.
