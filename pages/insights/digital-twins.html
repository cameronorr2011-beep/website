Digital twins for living cultures: models with humility

Jun 20269 min readEducational

A "digital twin" is a simulation that runs alongside a real system, fed by live measurements, used to forecast what comes next. The idea comes from engineering, where machines are predictable enough to model faithfully. Biology is different — and the difference is the whole story.

What a twin does

For a photobioreactor, a twin combines a few well-understood pieces: a growth model with nutrient limitation (Droop-type quota dynamics), a light-response curve, and self-shading corrections as the culture becomes denser. Feed it current measurements — optical density, temperature, pH, light — and it projects the trajectory forward: "at this rate, the culture crosses the target density on Thursday."

What a twin cannot do

It cannot see contamination your sensors missed. It cannot know what changed in the medium between calibrations. Its parameters were fit to one set of conditions and will quietly drift out of validity as conditions change. A twin is a hypothesis about your culture, not a mirror of it.

The humility checklist

  • State uncertainty — a forecast without error bars is a guess wearing a lab coat.
  • Stay legible — if a model can't explain itself, it can't be audited.
  • Verify before acting — no model output should reach hardware without deterministic checks.
  • Record the outcome — every prediction should be compared with what actually happened.

Why bother, then?

Because even an imperfect model is transformative for experiments. It turns a culture from a black box into something you can reason about before touching it. The goal isn't a perfect oracle — it's a useful, honest instrument that gets better with every recorded experiment. That is the version we are building.
