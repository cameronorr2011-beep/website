A small bench that grows one thing well.

Orr Biologicals cultures marine microalgae in United States, and sells it live — to hatcheries feeding bivalve larvae, to labs that need a clean starter, and to anyone who wants a litre of phytoplankton growing on a desk.

Why it exists

Live algae is a perishable, awkward, deeply unglamorous product. The big suppliers sell it by the litre to commercial hatcheries and the hobby market gets a bottle of concentrate with a use-by date. In between, there was nothing: no small quantities, no strains grown to a date you chose, and very little plain explanation of what the organism needs.

That gap is the whole business. We are not trying to be an algal paste plant. The plan is to grow a small number of strains properly, count every batch, ship it inside a week of harvest, and answer the email that arrives when a culture turns brown.

It started the way most small laboratories do: a student, a windowsill, a bottle of seawater and a culture that crashed twice before it lived. The kit on this site is the kit that would have saved those first two attempts.

Culture quality, without the adjectives

There is no certificate on the wall and no accreditation to cite. What there is, is a method, applied every batch, with the results written down.

  1. Unialgal, not axenic

    Cultures are started from a confirmed single strain and maintained with sterile technique, but they are not bacteria-free and we do not claim they are. Axenic cultures are a laboratory product at a different price; hatcheries do not need them.

  2. Counted, not estimated

    Haemocytometer, four grids, averaged — the figure on the label. If a batch counts low it is held back and re-grown, which costs a shipment but not a customer's spawn.

  3. Seven days, not stock rotation

    Cultures are grown to a ship date. Nothing is pulled from a fridge, because a refrigerated culture is a stalled culture, and a stalled culture does not divide in your tank.

  4. Contamination is a fail, not a discount

    Any foreign cell — ciliate, cyanobacterial filament, an unexpected diatom — fails the batch. The vessel is sterilised and the culture is discarded. Selling it cheap would cost more in reputation than it returns in cash.

What we are not

A bench-scale producer cannot be everything, and pretending otherwise wastes a buyer's time. We are not a paste plant, we do not hold a culture collection, and we cannot supply a hatchery running thousands of litres a day at a price that competes with a commercial algae facility.

We also do not sell anything we have not grown. If a strain is not available on your date, you will hear that before you hear a price.

What that means for you

Small volumes, short lead times on the strain we run continuously, a written quote within a business day, and a replacement if a culture arrives dead. For hatcheries that is usually a backup strain, a research spawn, or a batch to carry a tank through a week when the main supply is late.

For everyone else it is a bottle of live algae and a protocol that was tested on someone who had never grown any.
