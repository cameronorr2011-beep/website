04 / How we keep them

Citizen science,
one jar at a time.

Home fermentation is ordinary, forgiving and occasionally wrong — and that makes it perfect citizen science: anyone with a jar can repeat what we did and check what we saw. These three habits make it easier to tell a culture that is working from one that has quietly gone off.

01

Clean, then dry.

Wash jars, lids and spoons in hot soapy water and let them dry. You do not need a sterile field; a clean, dry vessel is enough for a home culture.

02

Keep conditions steady.

Most failures are a change in temperature, feeding or airflow. Keep the jar out of direct sun, away from draughts, and give it a predictable spot.

03

Write it down.

Date, flour or tea, temperature, what you changed, what you saw. A line a day turns a vague feeling into something you — and anyone else — can actually compare.

05 / Advanced technologies

Our inventions,
kept in a separate cabinet.

The kitchen notes are for anyone. This cabinet is the workshop, and everything in it is ours: Algaephyte, for growing and understanding cultures at scale, and Cyanoflow, for looking closely at single cells. Both grew directly out of lessons learned at this counter — and both remain research concepts in development, not products you can buy.

A–01

Algaephyte

Our culture-scale invention: sensors, a camera and a physics-informed growth model brought into one clearer view of a changing culture. Illustrative scene — a proposed instrument and its readings, not a deployed instrument.

Our invention · in development
A–02

Cyanoflow

Our single-cell invention: a proposed way to separate, photograph and follow individual cells so their differences are not averaged away. Design goals and illustrative imagery, not validated hardware performance.

Our invention · in development

Algaephyte and Cyanoflow are Orr Biologicals’ inventions, in active research and development. No validated hardware performance and no commercial deployment is claimed. The research approach defines how observations, models and proposals are labelled; developer notes describe what exists today.

07 / Open by default

Readable, open,
and reachable.

The site is built to be read by as many people as possible: real headings, keyboard focus that is visible, text that does not depend on colour alone, and motion that stops when you ask it to. The whole site — copy, styles and build scripts — is MIT-licensed open source, so anyone can read exactly how it was made, or make their own counter notebook.

If a page is hard to use with your setup, that is a bug. Write to us and we will fix it.

Developer notes ↗Research approach ↗
