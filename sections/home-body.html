04 / How we work

Citizen science,
one jar at a time.

Home fermentation is ordinary, forgiving and occasionally wrong — and that makes it perfect citizen science: anyone with a jar can repeat what we did and check what we saw. Three habits make it easier to tell a culture that is working from one that has quietly gone off.

01

Clean, then dry.

Wash jars, lids and spoons in hot soapy water and let them dry. You do not need a sterile field; a clean, dry vessel is enough for a home culture.

02

Keep conditions steady.

Most failures are a change in temperature, feeding or airflow. Keep the jar out of direct sun, away from draughts, and give it a predictable spot.

03

Write it down — in the open.

Date, flour or tea, temperature, what you changed, what you saw. A line a day turns a vague feeling into something you — and anyone who reads these MIT-licensed notes — can actually compare.

05 / Inventions

Our inventions,
in the open.

The applications are for anyone who finds them useful. The workshop is different: everything here is ours, and our own to prove. Algaephyte, for growing and understanding cultures at scale, and Cyanoflow, for looking closely at single cells. Both remain research concepts in development — designs, not deployments, and no commercial availability is claimed.

A–01

Algaephyte

Our culture-scale invention: sensors, a camera and a physics-informed growth model brought into one clearer view of a changing culture. Illustrative scene — a proposed instrument and its readings, not a deployed instrument.

Cyanoflow

Our single-cell invention: a proposed way to separate, photograph and follow individual cells so their differences are not averaged away. Design goals and illustrative imagery, not validated hardware performance.

Algaephyte and Cyanoflow are Orr Biologicals’ inventions, in active research and development. No validated hardware performance and no commercial deployment is claimed. The research approach defines how observations, models and proposals are labelled; developer notes describe what exists today.

07 / Open by default

Open source,
by default.

Open-source biotechnology for all means the protocols are readable, the site is readable, and both are yours to reuse: real headings, keyboard focus that is never hidden, text that does not depend on colour alone, and motion that stops when you ask it to. The whole site — copy, styles and build scripts — is MIT-licensed open source.

If a page is hard to use with your setup, that is a bug. Write to us and we will fix it.

Developer notes ↗Research approach ↗
  • 01Keyboard first. Every control is reachable, and focus is never hidden.
  • 02Contrast checked. Body text and controls are tested against the faded forest background.
  • 03Motion optional. Animations stop under reduced-motion preferences.
  • 04Honest labelling. Generated imagery is marked as generated, and nothing is passed off as a photograph of a real measurement.
  • 05MIT-licensed. Site code, protocol notes and build scripts — yours to read, run and reuse.
