Applications · Cultivation

Spirulina, kept simply.

Spirulina is a warm, alkaline water culture — a cyanobacterium, not a pond weed — that rewards patience, light and a notebook more than equipment.

Know the organism first

Arthrospira platensis (marketed as “spirulina”) is a filamentous cyanobacterium that evolved in alkaline lakes. It is not the same organism as the spirally coiled Spirulina sensu stricto, and it is definitely not the green growth on any random pond — a green bottle is suggestive, never conclusive. Keep your inoculum from a documented culture, not from the wild.

What spirulina is not

It is not a protein shake you grow in a jam jar, not proof of safety by appearance, and not a licensed drug or supplement. Home cultures are a citizen-science exercise with a harvestable by-product; identity, purity and safety need laboratory verification. Treat homegrown biomass as an experiment, never as a tested supplement — especially if immunocompromised.

Safety, plainly. Spirulina is a cyanobacterium; wild blooms can contain cyanotoxins. Do not harvest from unverified natural waters. Do not feed homegrown culture to infants or as medicine. Educational guide — not food-safety certification, identity verification or medical advice.
