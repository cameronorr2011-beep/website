Applications · Spirulina / Protocol

Your first culture.

Four weeks, not four days. Spirulina rewards a weekly rhythm and a written record far more than gadgets.

Before you start

Obtain a documented starter — a small live culture from a reputable supplier or a gifted culture with provenance you trust. Do not attempt “wild spirulina” from ponds; you cannot verify species or toxins, and the containment rules for culturing unknown organisms belong in an institution, not a kitchen.

Set-up

  1. Medium: a Zarrouk-type bicarbonate base (roughly 16 g sodium bicarbonate per litre, with trace nutrients) — see our medium walk-through. Make a litre, dissolve well, rest at room temperature overnight.
  2. Vessel: clean wide-mouth glass bottle or flat flask, loosened cap, out of direct sun but in a bright, warm spot (25–30 °C ideal).
  3. Inoculate: 10–20% v/v from the live culture. Mark the fill line and date it.
  4. Daily: 30 seconds of swirling. Weekly: check colour against the mark, note temperature, top up evaporated water with bicarbonate solution (not plain water) if the culture has dropped below the line.

What weeks 1–4 look like

WeekTypical state
1Colour barely changes; filaments settle between mixes; nothing looks like failure yet.
2Noticeable deepening to blue-green; first hint of a swirl when mixed.
3–4Opaque forest-green; steady growth; first partial harvest (strain through fine cloth) if density is heavy.

Doubling time is measured in days, not hours. If nothing visibly improves after two weeks, check temperature first — cold is the most common silent failure.

Not food-safety certification. Identity and purity cannot be confirmed by eye. Homegrown biomass is an educational harvest; treat it that way.
