Applications · Spirulina / Observation

Reading the culture.

Four field signs carry most of the information: colour, foam, sediment and smell. None of them certifies anything — they tell you where to look next.

Colour

Healthy Arthrospira is a deep blue-green (“forest green”). Olive or browning tones suggest ageing cells, light stress or declining alkalinity; a pale, watery green means too little pigment density or too much self-shading; grey turbidity hints at contamination or debris. Colour tracks health only roughly — never trust it alone.

Foam and surface

Gentle swirling foam that settles quickly is normal. Persistent greasy, foul-smelling foam, or a skin of mucus-like material, is not — those point to contamination and the culture should be isolated, not harvested.

Sediment and clarity

Filaments settling between mixes are fine; a thick permanent layer on the bottom with a clear top means the working volume is smaller than it looks. Swirl daily and keep the culture shallow enough that light reaches most cells — a flat tray beats a tall bottle.

Smell

Earthy, grassy, faintly ocean-like is typical. Rotten, sewage or putrid smells mean discard the culture — do not “treat” it with bicarbonate and continue.

When it goes wrong

For crashes, contamination crashes and restarts, see our troubleshooting notes. The honest summary: microscope or discard — there is no safe eyeball test, only exclusion decisions.

Not identity or safety verification. Visual signs never certify species identity, purity or the absence of cyanotoxins. Only laboratory analysis does. When in doubt, discard.
