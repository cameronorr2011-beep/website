Applications · Kombucha / Protocol

The first ferment, step by step.

One reproducible loop, logged. If you can make tea, you can make kombucha — the skill is noticing the batch.

Protocol

  1. Boil water, dissolve ~50–70 g sugar per litre, add black or green tea (1–2 tbsp leaves per litre), steep 10–15 min, then remove the leaves and cool completely. Never add a live culture to hot tea.
  2. Pour into clean glass. Add your SCOBY and ~10–15% starter liquid from a previous healthy batch (free acidity from the start suppresses the wrong organisms).
  3. Cover with tight-weave cloth or paper and a band — the culture breathes but dust and insects stay out.
  4. Rest at roughly 21–30 °C, out of direct sun, for 7–10 days. Taste from day 5: stop when the sweetness has dropped but not vanished.
  5. pH at or below ~3.5 is the conventional finish line. A basic pH strip is enough.

Bottling

Strain into pressure-safe bottles (swing-top or PET that can take carbonation), optionally with a spoon of juice or tea for a light second ferment, 1–3 days at room temperature. Open daily over the sink; glass bombs are a real failure mode. Refrigerate to halt the ferment.

What you will actually see

A new pellicle forms and thickens on the surface — that is cellulose and it is supposed to be there. Brown fibrils hanging into the liquid are normal yeast strands. Bubbling, a faint vinegar aroma and a fizz on the tongue arrive mid-ferment. None of this certifies safety; it describes a healthy-looking process, not a lab result.

Not food-safety certification. Worried about a batch? Do not taste “just to check”. Discard and start fresh with clean equipment and new starter liquid.

Sources

  1. Jayabalan R, et al. “A Review on Kombucha Tea.” Compr Rev Food Sci Food Saf, 2014. DOI: 10.1111/1541-4337.12073.
  2. Watawana MI, et al. “Health, wellness, and safety aspects of the consumption of kombucha.” Journal of Chemistry, 2015. DOI: 10.1155/2015/591869.
